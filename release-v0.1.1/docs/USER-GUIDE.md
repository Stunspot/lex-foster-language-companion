# Use Lex for learning and communication

Lex works best when language is attached to a person, purpose, and moment. You may begin with sparse input; the conversation should reveal only the context that changes the work.

## Learn through a real mission

Ask for the situation you need:

- prepare for a family dinner;
- understand a game or hobby community;
- rehearse a job interview;
- read a story or news passage;
- handle travel, shopping, housing, or school;
- write to a colleague or friend;
- recover from a misunderstanding.

A mission normally moves through encounter, attempt, focused feedback, repair, variation, and later retrieval. Lex may compress the cycle when you need a message immediately.

### Adjust the challenge

Say what helps:

- “Give me support, but make me produce the final sentence.”
- “Stay in the target language unless I ask.”
- “Do not interrupt fluency; give me two corrections at the end.”
- “Correct everything in this paragraph and explain the pattern.”
- “Use IPA only when an ordinary sound cue is not enough.”
- “Make the next version harder by changing the relationship.”

The current request supersedes an older saved preference.

## Rehearse a conversation

Name your role, the other person, and the desired outcome. Lex should play a plausible counterpart rather than feed you both sides of the exchange.

After a full turn, ask for:

- the one error that most changed meaning or social effect;
- a more natural alternative;
- a changed cue that requires the same skill;
- a recap of what you did independently and with support.

## Understand a correction

A correction should answer three questions:

1. What did the listener likely understand?
2. Which feature changed the meaning, tone, or naturalness?
3. Can you use the repair in a new utterance?

If the explanation becomes larger than the live need, ask Lex to reduce it to one contrast and one retry.

## Work with pronunciation or script

Without audio, Lex can prepare articulation, stress, rhythm, tone, syllable structure, script recognition, romanization, and minimal contrasts. Lex cannot assess sound that was not supplied.

With audio-capable hosts, keep the evidence boundary visible: recording quality, background noise, accent familiarity, and speech recognition may limit the judgment.

An accent is not a defect by itself. State whether your goal is intelligibility, lower listener effort, a local target, performance, or personal preference.

## Use culture and protocol

Ask how a form may sound in the named community and relationship. Useful guidance distinguishes grammar, genre convention, community tendency, relationship choice, and personal preference.

Request alternatives by effect:

- warmer;
- more deferential;
- more direct;
- more local to a named community;
- easier to repair if misunderstood.

If the answer describes an entire nationality as one personality, ask Lex to locate the claim by place, community, generation, role, and setting.

## Continue across sessions

A saved learner profile is optional. Use it when the benefit of resumption exceeds the privacy and maintenance cost. See [Learner state](LEARNER-STATE.md).

Without a profile, ask for a compact session recap and paste it into the next conversation.

## Know when the turn is complete

A useful tutoring turn ends with language you can use, a successful repair, a distinction you can now recognize, or a clear next retrieval cue. A useful translation turn ends with a target fit to the known brief, protected material intact, local uncertainty visible, and required human authority preserved.
